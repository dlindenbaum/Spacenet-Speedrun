SpaceNet 2 · Las Vegas · WorldView-3 · 22 Oct 2015

Nine years after SpaceNet 2, one model maps a city strip for under a dollar

In 2017 the winning entry found building footprints at F1 0.693. A single model trained in 53 minutes now scores 0.755, and it has mapped all 157,339 buildings in the Vegas image strip.

Scroll to follow the project. The map on the right streams the original satellite strip and every vector layer as you go. Use the panel to switch layers and explore on your own.

01 The 2017 challenge

650-pixel chips from four cities

SpaceNet 2 cut 0.3 m WorldView-3 imagery of Las Vegas, Paris, Shanghai and Khartoum into 650 × 650 px chips, about 200 m on a side, with hand-drawn building outlines. The official test labels were never released, so this project holds out 10 % of the public training chips as validation. No model ever trains on them.

Scoring is the challenge's own: a predicted outline counts only if it overlaps a labelled building by IoU ≥ 0.5, one to one. F1 is computed per city and averaged.

02 Zero-shot

Off-the-shelf models, no SpaceNet training

Vision-language models can't draw footprints. Their boxes are too loose for IoU 0.5. Used as prompts for SAM 2.1, though, Gemma 4's boxes reach 0.462. Given perfect boxes, SAM 2.1 scores 0.970, so the problem is finding buildings, not outlining them.

SAM 3 with the single word "building" scores 0.642 on all 1,037 validation chips. That's level with 2017's second place and 0.05 behind the winner, with zero SpaceNet labels.

03 Supervised

A one-hour model beats the 2017 winner in every city

The model is a U-Net with an ImageNet-22k ConvNeXt-B encoder, plain RGB input and three outputs (building, border, building-to-building contact), split into instances by watershed. It trained for 53 minutes on one A100, costing about $2.20.

It scores 0.755 overall against XD_XD's 0.693. The gains are largest in the hard cities: Shanghai +0.11, Khartoum +0.07.

04 Head to head

Rerunning the 2017 winner, code unchanged

XD_XD's weights were never released, so his original Keras 1 code was retrained on the same split. It runs on PyTorch through a compatibility shim, with 2017 OpenStreetMap layers rebuilt from the May 2017 planet file. Paris and Khartoum finished:

  • 0.697Paris, XD_XD retrained. Our model: 0.807
  • 0.512Khartoum, XD_XD retrained. Our model: 0.611

On identical tiles the 2026 model leads by about 0.10. SAM 3, with no training, lands within 0.02 of the retrained winner in both cities.

05 What doesn't matter

The 2017 winner's extra inputs no longer help

  • ±0.0048-band multispectral vs RGB. Extra bands started at zero weight; every 95 % interval contains 0, Khartoum included.
  • +0.005Post-processing, searched with cross-validation over 36 settings, dilation and minimum area.
  • −0.039DINOv3 satellite-pretrained ViT-L in place of ConvNeXt-B, with 3× the parameters.

The gain comes from a pretrained convolutional encoder plus an output that separates touching buildings. Colour bands, OpenStreetMap layers and ensembles add nothing measurable.

06 How general

Training on more SpaceNets makes one model general

Adding the SN4 (Atlanta, 27 look angles), SN6 (Rotterdam) and SN8 (Louisiana, Germany) training sets costs 0.017 on SN2 and roughly doubles F1 elsewhere. Test areas are held out by location, and SN6 was re-split geographically after an overlap audit caught leakage.

Show as table

New cities still need local labels. A model trained on SN4, SN6 and SN8 alone scores 0.325 on the SN2 cities, about half of zero-shot SAM 3.

07 Open building data

Overture matches SpaceNet in Vegas, and only there

Overture Maps merges OpenStreetMap, Microsoft ML Buildings and Google Open Buildings. In Vegas it agrees with SpaceNet's labels at F1 0.80, so here it is a usable second reference.

Elsewhere it scores 0.10–0.19 because the conventions differ. Paris footprints are offset from the rooftops in off-nadir imagery. Shanghai's dataset draws whole terraces as one shape. Khartoum's OpenStreetMap maps walled compounds instead of the buildings inside.

08 The whole strip

157,339 buildings, seamless, in 30 minutes

The model ran over the entire strip, about 14 × 16 km, read straight from SpaceNet's public cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs. It worked in 1,300 px windows and kept each building only where its centre falls in the window's core. That includes 39,595 buildings outside any SpaceNet chip.

  • 0.897F1 against SpaceNet labels on the 386 held-out Vegas chips. The per-chip result was 0.892.
  • 0.825agreement with Overture outside SpaceNet's area, against 0.829 inside. Accuracy holds on unlabelled ground.
